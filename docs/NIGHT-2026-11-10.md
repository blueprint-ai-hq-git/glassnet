# 2026-11-10, the whole day in one page

Ten arms were trained or measured today. Seven of them lost, and the losses are the useful part. Every number below
was measured on the fair (matched conversation-hash) split unless it says otherwise, and every one names its file.

## Where the day started
The best honest talk model scored **25.52%** (`lens/runs/MATCHED-D512.txt:65`), but its 36 named concepts only decided
**7.3%** of its answers (`READABLE-PATH-EXPLAINS-7PCT.md`), and its accuracy depended heavily on a "copy path" that
echoes words from the input window.

Two problems, which the day separated cleanly:
- the **loop**: the model repeats itself and cannot answer anything;
- the **content**: even when it does not loop, it does not know anything.

## Problem 1, the loop: four levers, four failures
| lever | what it did | result |
|---|---|---|
| symbol mask | banned punctuation from the copy path | symbols 8.7% -> 0.0%, loop moved to words, 4-grams 54.0% -> 47.6% |
| decode guard | blocked repeated 4-grams at generation | distinct 4-grams 39.3% -> 100%, text still meaningless |
| copy penalty | charged the model for echoing | ep1 +2.06 pp, previous-token echo -14.2 pp, gone by ep3 |
| exposure bias | trained on its own rollouts | ep1 looked best of the day, final **19.94%** vs 25.52% (**-5.58 pp**) |

Three explanations died by measurement: corpus repetition (math/code has MORE repeated tokens and loops LESS),
boilerplate density, and memorisation (**0.0%** of generated 16-grams appear verbatim in 38,829,567 training windows).
Then a fourth died: confidence sharpening (baseline entropy **rises** 4.19 -> 5.02 while its in-window rate **falls**
75.0% -> 70.8%).

What survived: **90%+ of emitted tokens are already in the window by step nine**. The loop is feedback at decoding
time.

## I had to correct myself twice
1. I published that the loop comes from the copy path, because the concept path held symbol probability at 0.007-0.008
   while the copy path climbed to 0.965. Then the concept-only model, with **no copy path at all**, looped **worse**:
   symbols 27.8% vs 8.7%, longest run 23 vs 3, distinct 4-grams 19.8% vs 54.0%, copy rate **100%** by step 17. The copy
   path carried the loop in one model; it is not its cause.
2. I guessed single-epoch wins meant something. Two levers looked like wins at epoch 1 and were behind by epoch 3.
   Single-epoch comparisons in this setup are not evidence.

## Problem 2, content and readability: the real finding of the day
**Capacity is not the answer.** d=768 (29.42M params, +89%) scored **26.02%** vs 25.52%, i.e. **+0.50 pp**, inside the
measured 0.26-3.07 pp seed spread, and the trainer flags it under-trained at 2.18 tokens per parameter
(`CAPACITY-VERDICT.md`). Worse, the extra capacity went to the unreadable path: concept agreement with the model's real
answers fell **8.2% -> 4.3%**.

**Readability is affordable in accuracy.** With the copy path removed so the 36 concepts are the only route to an
answer: **15.41% / 18.70% / 20.30%**, i.e. **5.22 pp** behind the copy-path model - not the 18.50-19.17 pp it costs to
retrofit concepts onto a copy-trained model (`READABLE-ARM-VERDICT.md`).

**But it is expensive in honesty.** Same gate, same 800 matched windows (`CONCEPT-LABEL-AUDIT.md`):

```
                        readable-only    copy-path
concepts passing           30/36           36/36
mean AUROC                 0.6886          0.8695
mean fire rate             47.4%           28.3%
```

Six concepts fail, and **all six fail only in the readable model**: `mentions_body` 0.4275, `mentions_a_name` 0.4662,
`mentions_sport` 0.4774, `mentions_a_quantity` 0.5435, `mentions_family` 0.5437, `is_a_list` 0.5471. Every formula
passes when the concepts only have to describe. They break when the concepts have to carry the whole prediction, and
they break by firing on about half of all windows - becoming a shared code rather than 36 separate descriptions.

## Where that leaves the project, honestly
Two real options, both now measured:
- **honest monitor**: 25.52% accuracy, 36/36 honest concepts, but `explain()` describes only 4-8% of decisions;
- **concepts decide**: 20.30% accuracy, 30/36 honest concepts, and `explain()` is true by construction.

The middle ground - both at once - has not appeared in any measurement so far. That is the open problem, and it is a
design problem, not a longer-training problem.
